# TOWN OF HEREVILLE, VIRGINIA
## Mayor's Brief — Video Script
## Hereville Town Council Special Session
## Elementary School Consolidation
**Document No.:** HVL-SCRIPT-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Runtime Target:** 4:30 – 5:00  
**Format:** Single speaker, seated at council table or standing at podium. Town seal visible. Professional but not slick — this is a small town.

---

> *PRODUCTION NOTE: The Mayor is not one of the five council members. This video is pre-recorded and plays on the main screen at the start of the session. Tone is formal but plain-spoken. No teleprompter feel. This person knows Hereville and is leveling with the room.*

---

### [OPEN — MAYOR ON CAMERA, HEREVILLE SEAL VISIBLE BEHIND]

Good evening. I'm Mayor [NAME], and I want to take a few minutes before we begin tonight's session to make sure everyone in this room — and everyone watching — understands what we're actually being asked to decide.

This is not a routine meeting.

The Hereville City Schools have asked this council to approve, in principle, the closure of Southside Elementary School. If we vote yes tonight, Southside closes at the end of this school year. One hundred and ninety-four children find a new school in September. The food pantry, the community center, the church that's used that gymnasium for eleven years — they find somewhere else to go. Or they don't.

That's what a yes vote means.

A no vote means we keep both schools open. It also means we do not close a $400,000 annual gap in our budget — a gap that exists on top of a structural deficit this town has been running for four years. I want to be honest with you: we don't have a plan B for that money. Not yet.

---

### [BEAT — SLIGHT PAUSE]

I've lived in Hereville my whole life. I've watched this town change. I remember when Main Street had a hardware store, two diners, a pharmacy, and a movie theater. I remember when the bypass opened and the traffic just... stopped coming through. I've watched good people try to reverse that and mostly not succeed.

I'm not telling you that to be discouraging. I'm telling you that because I want this council to make tonight's decision with clear eyes about what Hereville is and what it isn't.

We are a town of four thousand people with a shrinking tax base, aging infrastructure, and a school system that's lost nearly a quarter of its students in ten years. We are also a town with people who have lived here for generations, who remember what was here before, and who still believe this place is worth fighting for.

Both of those things are true at the same time.

---

### [BEAT]

The documents in front of you tonight — and there are a lot of them — will show you the numbers. I want to add a few things the numbers don't show.

Southside Elementary sits in a neighborhood that has already absorbed more than its share of hard decisions made in rooms like this one. Urban renewal took buildings off Garrison Street sixty years ago. The bypass took the through traffic. Every decade something else has been lost on the south side of this town. The people who live there know that pattern. They're watching to see if this council knows it too.

I'm not saying that means the school can't close. I'm saying it means the council has an obligation to look at this decision in that context, not just in the context of this year's budget.

---

### [BEAT]

A few things I'm asking you to keep in mind tonight.

First: the fiscal problem is real, and it does not go away if we vote no. Closing Southside buys us time. It doesn't buy us a solution. Whatever you decide tonight, this council will be back in this room next year facing the same structural question.

Second: there are things this proposal does not address that I think we owe the public an answer on. What happens to the food pantry? What happens to the community center? What happens to the staff — specifically the people who work with English-learning students and their families? The superintendent's proposal says those are not the school district's problem. I'd like to hear this council's view on whether they're ours.

Third: there is at least one option that has not been formally studied — adjusting the attendance zone boundary rather than closing a school. I'm not advocating for it. I'm noting that the district considered it and set it aside without showing us the analysis. You're entitled to ask for that before you vote.

---

### [BEAT — DIRECT TO CAMERA]

This council was elected to make decisions like this one. Not easy ones. Not ones where all the costs fall on people who can absorb them. The hard ones, where reasonable people disagree, where the math and the history and the community all point in different directions, and someone has to decide.

That's you tonight.

The public comment period will follow this brief. You'll hear from your neighbors. Then you'll deliberate. And then you'll vote.

I trust this council to do that work seriously.

Let's get started.

---

### [FADE OUT — TOWN SEAL]
