# TOWN OF HEREVILLE, VIRGINIA
## Public Comment Video Scripts
## Hereville Town Council Special Session — November 4, 2024
**Document No.:** HVL-SCRIPT-002  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Total Runtime Target:** 8:00 – 10:00  
**Format:** Each speaker approaches a public comment podium in the council chambers. Single camera. Name and address appear as lower-third graphic at start of each comment. Real people, not actors if possible — or actors directed toward naturalism, not performance.

---

> *PRODUCTION NOTE: These five speakers represent a cross-section of the community. They are not balanced in the sense of two-for, two-against, one-neutral. They are balanced in the sense that each one is telling a true thing. The council should feel the weight of all five before they deliberate. Speakers are listed in the order they appear.*

---

## SPEAKER 1: DARNELL WASHINGTON
**Lower third:** Darnell Washington, 14 Garrison Street
**Runtime target:** 75 seconds
**Character:** 58 years old. Lifelong south side resident. Works maintenance at the county hospital. Not a public speaker by nature. Came because his daughter asked him to. He's going to say what he came to say and sit down.

---

*[Approaches podium. Sets a folded piece of paper down, looks at it once, then mostly looks up.]*

My name is Darnell Washington. I live on Garrison Street, right down from the school.

My kids went to Southside. My grandkids go there now. My mother went to the school that used to be on that street before they tore it down.

I'm not going to stand here and tell you the budget doesn't matter. I know it matters. I'm not saying close your eyes to the numbers.

I'm saying: look at the whole picture. Every time something gets cut in this town, it gets cut on the south side. Every time. And then somebody explains why it made sense financially, and maybe it did. But after a while, the pattern is the pattern.

*[Pause.]*

I'm not calling anybody in this room a bad person. I'm saying the people on my street are watching what you do tonight, and they remember everything that came before it.

That's all I got.

*[Picks up paper, walks back to seat.]*

---

## SPEAKER 2: CAROLINE SHEN
**Lower third:** Caroline Shen, 241 Ridgemont Drive
**Runtime target:** 80 seconds
**Character:** 38 years old. North side resident. Two kids at Northside Elementary. Works as an accountant. Came because she genuinely supports consolidation and feels like that position isn't being represented fairly in the public conversation. She's a little defensive about it — she knows how she's going to look — but she believes what she's saying.

---

*[Approaches podium. Confident, prepared, slightly clipped.]*

Good evening. My name is Caroline Shen, I live on Ridgemont Drive, and I have two children at Northside Elementary.

I want to say something that I think needs to be said: supporting consolidation does not make you anti-south side. It does not make you indifferent to those families. I care about those kids. I just also care about fiscal reality.

We are running a deficit. Enrollment is down. We have a school in good condition with empty classrooms and a school with aging systems that needs over a million dollars in repairs. That is the situation.

I understand the community concerns. I do. But I've heard a lot of emotion at these meetings and not a lot of alternative proposals. If someone has a plan to close the budget gap that doesn't involve consolidation, I would genuinely like to hear it. I've been at three meetings. I haven't heard one yet.

*[Brief pause.]*

This is a hard decision. Hard decisions have costs. I hope the council makes the fiscally responsible one.

Thank you.

---

## SPEAKER 3: ROSA ELENA FUENTES
**Lower third:** Rosa Elena Fuentes, 67 Meridian Street  
**Runtime target:** 90 seconds  
**Character:** 44 years old. Born in Oaxaca, Mexico. Has lived in Hereville for nine years. Limited but functional English — she has prepared and practiced this statement. This costs her something to do. She knows it.

---

*[Approaches podium slowly. Has a single index card. Speaks carefully, with pauses to find words. Does not rush.]*

Good evening. My name is Rosa Elena Fuentes. I live on Meridian Street.

I have — three children. At Southside school. And I want to say —

*[Pause. Steadies herself.]*

When we come to Hereville, we do not know anyone. The school — the people at the school — they help us. Maria, the — the liaison — she call me when I don't understand the paper. She come to the school meeting with me so I can understand.

My son — he have trouble reading. The teacher, she find this early. She tell me. Now he get help. He is doing better.

*[Pause.]*

I do not know — what happen if the school close. I do not know if there is — someone like Maria — at the other school. Nobody tell me this yet.

I am asking you, please — before you decide — tell us what happen to our children. Tell us who will help us. 

*[Looks down at card, then back up.]*

Thank you for listening to me.

*[Steps back from podium.]*

---

## SPEAKER 4: PHIL ACKERMAN
**Lower third:** Phil Ackerman, 509 Blue Ridge Terrace
**Runtime target:** 70 seconds
**Character:** 67 years old. Retired. North side. Fiscally conservative, practically minded, not unkind but impatient with what he sees as sentimentality. He thinks the decision is obvious and he's a little frustrated it's taken this long. He's not wrong about everything.

---

*[Approaches podium. No notes.]*

Phil Ackerman, Blue Ridge Terrace. I'll be brief.

I've watched this town spend thirty years avoiding hard decisions. The bypass hurt us and instead of adjusting we just kept doing what we were doing and hoping things would turn around. They didn't.

Enrollment is down twenty-three percent in ten years. That's not a blip. That's a trend. And we've got two school buildings, one of them in good shape and one of them that needs a million dollars in repairs, and we're debating whether to keep them both open.

Close the school. Put the money toward something that helps this town grow. And then actually do the work of helping it grow, instead of spending our energy on preserving buildings we can't afford.

I feel for the families at Southside. I do. But feeling bad about a necessary decision doesn't make it unnecessary.

Thank you.

*[Steps back.]*

---

## SPEAKER 5: KENNETH MCALLISTER
**Lower third:** Kenneth McAllister, 88 Garrison Street
**Runtime target:** 85 seconds
**Character:** 72 years old. Black. Retired teacher — taught at Southside Elementary for 24 years before retiring in 2018. He is not angry. He is tired in a particular way that comes from having watched a long time. He speaks slowly, with authority. He does not raise his voice.

---

*[Approaches podium. Stands quietly for a moment before speaking.]*

My name is Kenneth McAllister. I taught at Southside Elementary for twenty-four years. I retired six years ago.

I've heard a lot of talk about numbers at these meetings. Enrollment numbers. Cost numbers. Capacity numbers. Numbers matter. I taught them to children for a long time.

Here's another number: 1966. That's when this district desegregated. That's when Garrison Street School — which sat three blocks from where I'm standing — was closed. The Black teachers weren't kept on. The building was eventually torn down.

I'm not saying tonight is 1966. I know it's not. I know the people in this room are not the people who made those decisions.

*[Pause.]*

I'm saying that Southside Elementary was built, in part, to serve a community that had already lost its school once. And the people in that community know that history, even if this council doesn't talk about it.

*[Pause.]*

I'm not here to tell you what to vote. I taught civics for twenty-four years. I believe in this process.

I'm here to make sure you know what you're voting on.

*[Nods slightly. Steps back.]*

- *Speaker 5 (Kenneth McAllister) should be the last voice before deliberations begin. His final line — "I'm here to make sure you know what you're voting on" — is the transition.*
- *No music between speakers. Brief black between each comment.*
- *Lower-third graphics: simple, white text on dark bar. Name and street address only.*
- *If using actors: Speaker 1 should not look like a public speaker. Speaker 4 should not look like a villain. Both are important.*
- *Running order is intentional. Do not reorder without revisiting the deliberation arc.*
