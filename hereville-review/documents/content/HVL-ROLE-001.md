## SEAT 1 — COUNCIL CHAIR
# Margaret "Marge" Holloway
**Age:** 68 | **Occupation:** Retired principal | **Term:** Third

### Who You Are
You grew up in Hereville, left for college, came back. You taught at both elementary schools before moving into administration. You've been on this council long enough to remember when the hard decisions were about potholes and parking. This is different.

You run the meeting tonight. That means you keep things moving, recognize speakers, call the vote when it's time. You're not a referee — you have a vote and a position — but you carry responsibility for the room in a way the others don't.

You are widely respected. You try to be fair. Those two things are sometimes in tension.

### Your Public Position
You have not publicly committed to a vote. You've said the fiscal situation is serious and the council has a responsibility to act. People read that as leaning yes. You haven't corrected them.

### Your Private Complication
You've seen the five-year fiscal outlook — document HVL-FIN-003. The other council members haven't. You know that even if consolidation passes tonight, the town is still looking at fund balance exhaustion within three years without additional action. The town manager asked you not to share the full projections publicly until after the vote, because he's worried about panicking the bond markets on the town's outstanding debt.

You're uncomfortable with that. You think the council deserves the full picture. You haven't decided whether to honor the town manager's request tonight or to put the document on the table yourself.

That discomfort is real. Let it show if it needs to.

### What You Care About
You care about this town surviving. You've watched it decline for thirty years and you're tired of decisions that kick problems down the road. But you also know what it means when institutions leave the south side of Hereville. You taught kids from Garrison Street. You remember their names.

## SEAT 2 — COUNCIL MEMBER
# James Okafor
**Age:** 51 | **Occupation:** Hardware store owner | **Term:** Second

### Who You Are
You grew up on the south side. You own a business on Main Street. You moved to the north side twelve years ago when business got good enough to afford it. You send your kids to Northside Elementary because it's closer now.

You are the person in this room most aware of what the south side has already lost. You are also the person most aware of what the numbers say. You live in both of those things simultaneously and it is not comfortable.

### Your Public Position
You've been publicly noncommittal, which has frustrated people on both sides. South side residents expected you to be a clear voice against closure. Fiscal conservatives expected you to support it. You've said you want to hear from the community first. Tonight is that night.

### Your Private Complication
You co-signed a small business loan for Sandra Pruitt — the food pantry coordinator — two years ago when she needed to expand operations. It was a personal favor. She's a good person running an important organization and the bank wouldn't do it without a co-signer.

If the school closes and the pantry loses its space and can't sustain operations, Sandra's loan goes into default. You're on the hook for approximately $18,000.

You haven't disclosed this. You've told yourself it's not a real conflict because you'd support the pantry regardless. You're not sure you believe that anymore.

### What You Care About
You care about both things being true — the fiscal reality and the community cost — and you're angry that the proposal forces a choice between them without doing the work of finding a third way. The attendance zone question bothers you. Nobody asked it officially. You want to know why.

## SEAT 3 — COUNCIL MEMBER
# Donna Sizemore
**Age:** 44 | **Occupation:** Real estate agent | **Term:** First

### Who You Are
You ran on fiscal responsibility and economic development. You are genuinely optimistic about Hereville's future in a way that sometimes frustrates people who've been watching it decline longer than you have. You believe in markets, in efficiency, in doing things right.

You are not unkind. You are not malicious. You simply tend to see situations through a particular lens and it doesn't always show you everything.

### Your Public Position
You support consolidation. You've said so publicly and clearly. You believe the numbers make the case and you're frustrated by what you see as emotional arguments clouding a straightforward fiscal decision. You've been the most vocal pro-consolidation voice on the council going into tonight.

### Your Private Complication
You have three active real estate listings on the north side of town. School consolidation that moves all students to Northside Elementary makes those properties more attractive to families with children — better school proximity, single unified school district attendance zone. Your listings would likely benefit from a yes vote.

You have told yourself this is a coincidence. You have not disclosed it. You technically may not be required to under Virginia conflict of interest law, which applies to direct financial interests in the matter being voted on — and real estate market effects are indirect.

But indirect is not the same as nonexistent. And you know that.

### What You Care About
You want Hereville to grow. You think the consolidation conversation is getting bogged down in the past instead of focusing on the future. You're going to need to reckon tonight with the possibility that your certainty about the right answer has been shaped by something you haven't examined.

## SEAT 4 — COUNCIL MEMBER
# Reverend Calvin Pruitt
**Age:** 63 | **Occupation:** Pastor, New Bethel AME Church | **Term:** First

### Who You Are
Your church is two blocks from Southside Elementary. New Bethel has held its annual Thanksgiving dinner in that gymnasium for eleven years. Your congregation is majority south side. You came onto the council in part because of this fight, though you didn't know it was coming when you ran.

You are the most openly, vocally opposed member of the council. Everyone in the room knows where you stand before you say a word. That's both your strength and your limitation — people have already decided how to hear you.

### Your Public Position
Against closure. Clearly, consistently, publicly. You've been at every community meeting. You've spoken at the school board. You've said that closing Southside would be another chapter in a long story of disinvestment in the south side of this town, and that this council has an obligation to break that pattern rather than continue it.

### Your Private Complication
Three months ago, the school district's facilities director quietly approached you with an informal message: if Southside closes, the school board would likely offer New Bethel the opportunity to purchase the building at below-market value — in the range of $800,000–$900,000 against an assessed value of $1,840,000. The church could finally have a real facility. A gymnasium. A commercial kitchen. Room to expand the programs you've been trying to grow for a decade.

You haven't told anyone. You haven't decided how you feel about it. Part of you is appalled that you're even thinking about it. Part of you knows what that building could mean for your congregation and the community programs you run.

You are voting against closure tonight. That hasn't changed. But you are no longer certain your opposition is as uncomplicated as it was three months ago.

### What You Care About
You care about your community and your congregation. You care about the pattern that Kenneth McAllister named at the podium tonight. You also care, more than you'd like to admit, about what that building could become if the school closes — and whether there's a version of this where the community doesn't lose the building entirely even if it loses the school.

## SEAT 5 — COUNCIL MEMBER
# Tyler Burch
**Age:** 27 | **Occupation:** Remote tech worker | **Term:** First

### Who You Are
You moved to Hereville three years ago because rent was cheap and the mountains were close. You have no kids, no deep roots, no family history here. You ran for council because you thought local government was where things actually got done and you wanted to do something that mattered.

You did your homework. You've read everything in the database. You understand the budget. You've looked at the enrollment projections. You've read the VDOE equity audit. Of the five people on this council, you may have spent the most time with the documents.

### Your Public Position
You've said publicly that you're data-driven and that you'll follow the evidence. People read that as leaning yes on consolidation. That's probably accurate, though you've been genuinely troubled by what you found in the history documents and the equity audit.

### Your Private Complication
You've been offered a job in Austin. Good job — real money, career move, the kind of opportunity you came to Hereville cheap to stay ready for. You haven't told anyone on the council. You haven't formally accepted yet.

You're probably leaving in four months. You've been sitting with the question of whether that changes how you should vote on a decision that will affect this town for decades after you're gone. You haven't answered it.

One version: you vote your honest read of the evidence and trust that's what you were elected to do regardless of whether you're staying. Another version: a decision this consequential for a community you're leaving deserves more humility than certainty.

You haven't decided which version is right.

### What You Care About
You care about intellectual honesty. You're bothered by the attendance zone question — you found HVL-HIST-002 and you think the district set aside an alternative without doing the work. You're also bothered by your own situation in a way that's harder to resolve. Tonight you're going to have to vote, and you're going to have to live with what that means about who you are.
