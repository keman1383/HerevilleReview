### ABOUT THE VDOE EQUITY AUDIT PROGRAM

The Virginia Department of Education's Local Education Agency Equity Profile program was established in 2020 to provide Virginia school divisions with a standardized analysis of equity indicators across schools and student groups. Participation is voluntary. Hereville City Schools requested an equity profile review in spring 2022. The review was conducted between April and October 2022 and the final report was issued November 2022.

The profile examines indicators across five domains: Access, Climate, Outcomes, Resources, and Community Engagement.

---

### SUMMARY FINDINGS

The VDOE equity profile identified areas of strength and areas of concern across Hereville City Schools. The following summarizes key findings relevant to the elementary school consolidation discussion.

---

### DOMAIN 1: ACCESS

**Finding A-1: Differential Program Access**
The profile found that English Learner services are more robust at Southside Elementary than at Northside Elementary, reflecting the concentration of EL students in the Southside attendance zone. The report noted that EL students attending Northside receive services from a shared part-time specialist, while those at Southside have access to a full-time specialist and a bilingual family liaison. The report identified this as a resource disparity that advantaged EL students at Southside relative to those at Northside.

**Finding A-2: Gifted Identification Rates**
Black and Hispanic students are identified for gifted services at rates below their share of enrollment at both schools. District-wide, Black students represent 25.3% of enrollment but 14.1% of students identified as gifted. Hispanic students represent 16.7% of enrollment and 9.4% of students identified as gifted. The profile identified the gifted identification process as an area requiring review.

**Finding A-3: Special Education Placement**
The profile found no significant disparity in special education identification rates by race at the elementary level. It noted, however, that Southside Elementary does not operate a self-contained special education classroom, requiring students with more intensive needs to be transported to Northside. The report identified this as an access concern for Southside families.

---

### DOMAIN 2: CLIMATE

**Finding C-1: Family Engagement**
Survey data collected as part of the equity profile found significantly lower rates of reported family engagement with the school among Southside Elementary families compared to Northside. The report attributed this in part to language barriers, work schedule constraints among lower-income households, and, in some cases, concerns among immigrant families about interactions with government institutions.

The bilingual family liaison position at Southside was identified as a mitigating factor and a model for district-wide family engagement improvement.

**Finding C-2: Sense of Belonging**
Student survey data found comparable rates of reported school belonging at both elementary schools. The report noted this as a strength, particularly given the demographic and socioeconomic differences between the two schools.

---

### DOMAIN 3: OUTCOMES

**Finding O-1: Achievement Gap**
The profile documented a significant gap in SOL pass rates between Northside and Southside Elementary (see HVL-EDU-002 for current data). The report conducted a regression analysis controlling for student-level poverty indicators and found that the school-level achievement gap was substantially explained by differences in student economic status rather than school-level instructional quality differences.

The report's conclusion on this point: *"The achievement difference between Northside and Southside Elementary students is more accurately understood as an economic achievement gap than a school quality gap. Students at comparable economic levels perform similarly at both schools."*

**Finding O-2: Chronic Absenteeism**
The profile identified chronic absenteeism as an area of concern at Southside Elementary (then approximately 17%, compared to 11% at Northside). The report linked elevated absenteeism rates to housing instability, transportation challenges, and, for some families, reluctance to engage with institutions perceived as reporting to authorities.

---

### DOMAIN 4: RESOURCES

**Finding R-1: Per-Pupil Expenditure**
Per-pupil expenditure at Southside Elementary exceeds that at Northside when calculated on a cost-per-student basis, reflecting the fixed costs of operating a smaller school. The report noted this finding in the context of consolidation discussions then beginning at the district level, observing that per-pupil cost differences do not, by themselves, indicate resource inequity when student need levels differ between schools.

**Finding R-2: Facility Condition**
The profile noted the disparity in facility condition between the two schools (Good vs. Fair) and identified the aging HVAC system at Southside as a concern for student health and learning environment, particularly during periods of extreme heat in early fall and late spring.

---

### DOMAIN 5: COMMUNITY ENGAGEMENT

**Finding E-1: School as Community Anchor**
The profile identified Southside Elementary as functioning as a community anchor institution beyond its educational role, citing the food pantry, Hispanic Community Center, and church uses of the facility. The report noted: *"The concentration of community services at Southside Elementary reflects both the depth of community need in the surrounding area and the absence of alternative institutional infrastructure in the south side neighborhood."*

The report identified this function as relevant to any consolidation analysis but did not make a recommendation on consolidation.

**Finding E-2: Community Trust**
The profile found lower levels of institutional trust among south side residents relative to north side residents, based on community survey data. The report identified this as a legacy of historical experiences including urban renewal displacement and school desegregation, and noted that decisions perceived as removing resources from the south side neighborhood carried heightened risk of trust erosion.

---

### DISTRICT RESPONSE

Hereville City Schools accepted the equity profile findings and submitted a required response in March 2023. The district committed to: reviewing gifted identification procedures; exploring expansion of family liaison services; and including equity indicators in future facilities planning. The district's response did not address attendance zone boundaries.

A follow-up review of district progress against these commitments is scheduled for spring 2025.
