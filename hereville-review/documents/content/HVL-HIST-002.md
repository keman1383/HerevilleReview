### ZONING DISTRICT HISTORY — SELECTED TIMELINE

**1952 — First Comprehensive Zoning Ordinance**
Hereville adopted its first comprehensive zoning ordinance in 1952. The ordinance established residential zones differentiating single-family (R-1) from multi-family (R-2) uses. At adoption, the Garrison District and surrounding south side neighborhoods were zoned predominantly R-2, permitting higher-density residential development including rooming houses and multi-unit dwellings. North side residential areas were zoned R-1.

> *Planning records from 1952 do not document the rationale for the R-1/R-2 boundary placement. The boundary corresponded closely with the informal racial divide in residential occupancy patterns at the time of adoption.*

**1962–1971 — Urban Renewal Designations**
Urban renewal project boundaries encompassed approximately 14 blocks of the Garrison District, designated as a "blighted area" under the federal Urban Renewal Act. The designation enabled condemnation and demolition of structures within the project area. Post-clearance, the urban renewal area was rezoned to R-2 and partially to Public/Institutional use. Replacement housing was constructed on a portion of the cleared land; the remainder was never developed.

**1971 — Zoning Map Revision**
Following completion of urban renewal demolition, the town revised its zoning map. Several blocks in the Garrison District were redesignated from residential use to Public/Institutional or left without a primary use designation pending redevelopment that did not occur. These parcels remain in irregular zoning status in the current ordinance.

**1985–1990 — North Side Residential Expansion**
In anticipation of and following the Route 220 bypass construction, the town rezoned approximately 180 acres on the north side from Agricultural/Rural to R-1 Single-Family Residential. This action enabled the residential subdivision development that occurred between 1988 and 2005. No comparable rezoning or investment incentive was applied to the south side during this period.

**2006 — Comprehensive Rezoning**
Hereville completed a comprehensive zoning ordinance rewrite in 2006. The R-1/R-2 boundary on the south side was largely retained. The 2006 ordinance added a Mixed-Use (MU) overlay district along Main Street but did not extend it into the Garrison District.

### SCHOOL ATTENDANCE ZONE HISTORY

**Pre-Desegregation (prior to 1966)**
Hereville City Schools operated a formally segregated dual system. Garrison Street School served Black students. The white elementary school served all other students. Attendance was assigned by race, not geography.

**Desegregation and Zone Establishment (1966)**
Upon court-ordered desegregation, Hereville City Schools established geographic attendance zones for the first time. The single elementary school drew its attendance zone from the full town. Garrison Street School was closed.

**Northside Elementary Opening (1987)**
When Northside Elementary opened in 1987, the district established two attendance zones divided by Route 14. The northern zone encompassed the new residential subdivisions and the established north side neighborhoods. The southern zone encompassed the Garrison District and surrounding south side neighborhoods, including the areas most affected by urban renewal displacement.

The zone boundary placement was described in school board minutes as based on "natural geographic division and equitable distribution of enrollment." Enrollment at the time of opening was approximately 290 students at Southside and 340 at Northside.

**Current Attendance Zones**
The Route 14 boundary has not been formally reviewed or modified since 1987. Over the intervening 37 years, residential development patterns — concentrated on the north side — have produced increasing demographic divergence between the two zones without any adjustment to zone boundaries.

### ENROLLMENT DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS

The following table illustrates how the demographic composition of the two schools has changed since Northside Elementary opened, reflecting residential development and demographic change within fixed attendance zones.

| Year | Northside — White % | Northside — Black % | Southside — White % | Southside — Black % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1990 | 81.2% | 14.4% | 58.3% | 33.1% |
| 1995 | 79.6% | 15.1% | 54.2% | 35.8% |
| 2000 | 78.1% | 15.8% | 51.4% | 37.2% |
| 2005 | 76.4% | 16.2% | 48.8% | 38.4% |
| 2010 | 74.8% | 17.1% | 43.1% | 39.6% |
| 2015 | 73.2% | 17.8% | 39.4% | 40.1% |
| 2020 | 72.0% | 18.0% | 36.8% | 40.9% |
| 2024 | 71.1% | 18.1% | 34.0% | 41.2% |

> *The demographic divergence between the two schools has widened consistently over 37 years without any change in zone boundaries. This trend reflects the combined effect of: (1) north side residential development attracting higher-income, predominantly white households; (2) south side population decline concentrated among white households; and (3) growth in the Hispanic/Latino population concentrated in the south side attendance zone. The current demographic gap between the two schools is the widest it has been since Northside Elementary opened.*

### NOTE ON BOUNDARY ALTERNATIVES

The Virginia Department of Education's School Equity Guidelines (2021) identify attendance zone boundaries as one of several factors contributing to within-district school segregation. VDOE guidance recommends that districts review attendance zone boundaries when demographic divergence between schools exceeds 20 percentage points on any racial or ethnic category.

The current divergence between Northside and Southside Elementary on the Black or African American category is 23.1 percentage points (18.1% vs. 41.2%). The divergence on the Hispanic or Latino category is 13.1 percentage points (8.0% vs. 21.1%).

Hereville City Schools has not conducted a formal attendance zone review. No boundary adjustment has been proposed by the district in connection with the consolidation study.

> *It is noted that attendance zone boundary adjustment — as distinct from school closure — represents an alternative policy option that has not been formally analyzed or presented to the town council or school board as part of the current consolidation discussion.*
