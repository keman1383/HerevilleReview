# TOWN OF HEREVILLE, VIRGINIA
## Facilitator Guide — Complete Session
## Hereville Town Council Special Session
## Elementary School Consolidation
**Document No.:** HVL-FAC-001  
**For Facilitator Use Only — Not for Participant Distribution**

---

## OVERVIEW

**Total Session Time:** 50 minutes  
**Participants:** 5 (one per council seat)  
**Facilitator Role:** You are not in the simulation. You are the timekeeper, the GM failsafe, and the debrief leader. During the simulation you speak only to manage time or surface information participants have missed. During debrief you lead the conversation.

---

## SESSION TIMELINE

| Phase | Duration | What Happens |
|---|---|---|
| Role Setup | 5 min | Participants read role cards silently at stations |
| Mayor's Brief | 5 min | Video plays on main screen |
| Document Research | 5 min | Participants drill into background database |
| Public Comment Videos | 8–10 min | Five speakers play on main screen |
| Deliberation + Research | 15 min | Council deliberates; stations remain available |
| Vote + Debrief | 10 min | Vote recorded; debrief begins immediately |

**Hard stop at 50 minutes regardless of where deliberation is.**

---

## PHASE-BY-PHASE GUIDE

### PHASE 1: ROLE SETUP (Minutes 0–5)

Distribute role cards. Participants read silently. Do not allow discussion.

Say only this: *"Read your role card carefully. Your public position is what you've said publicly. Your private complication is yours to manage. You'll have five minutes."*

At the 5-minute mark, cut it off even if people are still reading. The pressure of incomplete information is intentional.

---

### PHASE 2: MAYOR'S BRIEF (Minutes 5–10)

Play HVL-SCRIPT-001 on the main screen. No introduction needed.

After the video ends, say: *"You have five minutes to review the background documents on your station before public comment begins."*

---

### PHASE 3: DOCUMENT RESEARCH (Minutes 10–15)

Participants drill into the database at their stations. Let them work. Do not guide them toward specific documents.

**Documents most likely to be missed in five minutes:**
- HVL-HIST-002 (attendance zone analysis — the third option)
- HVL-FIN-003 (five-year outlook — Marge's document)
- HVL-PROP-002 (town manager's review memo — the gaps in the proposal)

Note which participants find these. It matters for debrief.

---

### PHASE 4: PUBLIC COMMENT VIDEOS (Minutes 15–25)

Play HVL-SCRIPT-002 speakers in order on main screen. Brief black between each speaker. No facilitation during this phase.

If a participant tries to speak during the videos, say quietly: *"Public comment first. Then you'll have the floor."*

---

### PHASE 5: DELIBERATION (Minutes 25–40)

This is the heart of the session. Your job is to stay out of the way and watch.

**Open deliberation with:** *"Public comment is closed. The floor is open for council deliberation. Marge, as chair, whenever you're ready."*

Then sit down. The Marge player runs the room.

**What to watch for:**
- Does anyone raise the attendance zone alternative?
- Does anyone ask what happens to the food pantry specifically?
- Does anyone call for a delay or request additional information rather than voting yes or no?
- Does anyone disclose their private complication?
- Does Marge surface HVL-FIN-003 or stay silent about it?
- Does Tyler mention the Austin job?
- Does James mention the loan?
- Does anyone push back on Donna's certainty in a way that surfaces her conflict?
- Does Calvin's position shift in any visible way?

**GM Interventions — use sparingly, only if deliberation stalls completely:**

*Intervention A (if no one raises the history):* Pass a written note to the Marge player that reads: *"A community member has asked the council to address the historical context of decisions affecting the south side. Does the council wish to respond?"*

*Intervention B (if no one raises the food pantry):* Pass a note to any player: *"Sandra Pruitt, the food pantry coordinator, is in the audience and has asked whether the council will address the pantry's situation before the vote."*

*Intervention C (if deliberation dies entirely):* Say aloud: *"Council, you have eight minutes remaining before the vote."*

**Do not intervene more than once.** Silence is not a problem. Discomfort is productive.

---

### PHASE 6: VOTE AND DEBRIEF (Minutes 40–50)

At the 40-minute mark, say: *"Council, it's time to vote. Marge, please call the vote."*

The vote proceeds. Each council member votes yes, no, or abstain. Record publicly.

**After the vote is recorded — do not discuss the outcome yet. Move immediately to debrief.**

---

## DEBRIEF GUIDE

The debrief is where the learning lives. Give it the full remaining time. Do not rush it.

**Open with:** *"Step out of your roles. You're yourselves again."*

Then: *"Before we talk about the vote — what was it like to be in that room?"*

Let people talk. Don't rush to the structured questions.

---

### DEBRIEF QUESTIONS — USE SELECTIVELY, NOT ALL

**On the decision itself:**
- What information most changed how you were thinking?
- Was there information in the database that you wish you'd found sooner?
- Did anyone consider a motion other than yes or no — a delay, a condition, a request for more analysis? Why or why not?
- Was the attendance zone alternative ever on the table in your deliberation? Why did it come up or not come up?

**On the role experience:**
- How did your private complication affect how you participated?
- Did anyone disclose their complication? Why or why not?
- If you didn't disclose it, at what point did it feel like it was shaping your position without the others knowing?
- [To the Marge player specifically]: What did it feel like to know something the others didn't? Did you say anything? Why?

**On the racial and historical dimension:**
- Kenneth McAllister said "I'm here to make sure you know what you're voting on." What did you understand him to mean?
- Did the historical documents change how you thought about the fiscal documents?
- Darnell Washington said "the pattern is the pattern." Did your council address that? How?
- Caroline Shen said she hadn't heard an alternative proposal. Was that a fair characterization of the situation tonight?

**On civic decision-making:**
- What does it mean to make a good decision when there's no option that doesn't hurt someone?
- Who had power in this room tonight? Who didn't?
- What would a genuinely accountable process look like — not just a legal one?

**On the simulation itself:**
- What felt real? What felt false?
- What would you want to know that the documents didn't tell you?

## FACILITATOR NOTES — THINGS TO KNOW BEFORE YOU RUN THIS

**The right answer doesn't exist.** There are stronger and weaker arguments, more and less attentive readings of the documents, more and less honest performances of the roles. There is no correct vote. Do not imply otherwise in debrief.

**The private complications are not traps.** They're not there to catch people doing the wrong thing. They're there to make participants feel the texture of real governance — that almost everyone in a room like this is carrying something, and that the official process doesn't surface any of it.

**The attendance zone option is the most important thing nobody talks about.** It appears in three documents — HVL-HIST-002, HVL-PROP-001 Section 7, and HVL-PROP-002 Question 4 — and it appears in written comment #6. If participants don't raise it in deliberation, ask about it in debrief. It's the clearest example in the simulation of a decision being foreclosed before the public even got to weigh in.

**The racial dimension should not be the only takeaway.** It is an essential dimension. It is not the only one. The fiscal problem is real. The building condition is real. The enrollment trend is real. A debrief that collapses into "racism explains everything" has missed the complexity the simulation was designed to surface. Push back gently if this happens.

**Silence during deliberation is not failure.** Some groups will have ten seconds of silence and then someone will break it and do something remarkable. Wait it out.

**Watch James.** The food pantry loan is the most legally significant undisclosed conflict in the room. If James raises it, the debrief gets very interesting very fast. If he doesn't, that's worth discussing too.

**Watch Tyler.** The Austin job question is the most philosophically interesting private complication. It raises the question of whether temporary residents have full moral standing to make permanent decisions — which is a real question in civic life, not just a game mechanic.

**The food pantry coordinator's letter (Comment 7, HVL-COM-001) names a $2,400/month alternative location.** If participants vote yes without addressing the pantry, ask in debrief whether they think that's acceptable, and what a responsible yes vote would have included as a condition.

---

## COMMON FACILITATOR MISTAKES

**Rescuing the group from discomfort.** If the room is tense, let it be tense. That's the point.

**Over-facilitating deliberation.** Your GM interventions are a last resort. Use them once or not at all.

**Letting debrief become a verdict.** The debrief is not about whether the group voted correctly. It's about what they noticed, what they missed, and what that tells them about how civic decisions get made.

**Skipping the personal questions.** The role complications are the most pedagogically rich part of the simulation. Don't let debrief stay abstract. Ask people what it felt like.

**Running out of time on debrief.** If anything, end deliberation a minute early to protect debrief time. The vote matters less than the conversation after it.
