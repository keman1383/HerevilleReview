## Memorandum

**To:** Hereville Town Council  
**From:** R. Atkins, Town Manager

**Re:** Staff Review — Hereville City Schools Consolidation Proposal (HVL-PROP-001)  
**Distribution:** Town Council Members, Town Attorney, Public Record

---

### PURPOSE

This memorandum provides town staff observations on the consolidation proposal submitted by Hereville City Schools on September 9, 2024. Staff does not make a recommendation on whether to approve consolidation. That is a policy decision for elected officials. Staff does identify questions and gaps in the proposal that council members may wish to address before voting.

---

### OBSERVATIONS

**1. The fiscal projections are reasonable but not independently verified.**

The savings figures in the consolidation proposal (HVL-FIN-002) were prepared by the school district's own finance office. Town staff has reviewed these figures for internal consistency and finds them plausible. However, the town has not commissioned an independent fiscal review. Given that the projected savings are a primary rationale for consolidation, council may wish to request independent verification, particularly of the transition cost estimates, which carry significant uncertainty.

Staff notes that even with consolidation savings, the five-year fiscal outlook (HVL-FIN-003) projects fund balance exhaustion by FY2027–28. Council should not treat a yes vote on consolidation as resolving the town's fiscal position.

**2. The capacity plan is underdeveloped.**

The proposal identifies two options for addressing the enrollment overage at Northside (modular classrooms or redistricting) but does not commit to either. Combined enrollment of 481 students exceeds Northside's 420-student capacity. If redistricting is the preferred approach, the district estimates 18–24 months to achieve capacity compliance through attrition. During that period, Northside would operate over capacity. The proposal does not address how the school would function during this transition period or what the educational impact would be.

Council may wish to require the district to commit to a specific capacity resolution approach as a condition of approval, rather than leaving this to post-vote planning.

**3. The community services question is unresolved and the proposal declines to address it.**

Section 5 of the proposal acknowledges that the Community Partnership Agreement terminates upon school closure and that the district considers community service transition "outside the scope of the educational consolidation decision." Staff disagrees with this framing.

The food pantry, the Hispanic Community Center, and the church uses of the facility serve residents of Hereville. The town council is being asked to approve a decision that will displace these services. The fact that the school district does not consider this its problem does not mean it is not the council's problem.

Staff recommends that council condition any approval on the development of a community services transition plan, with specific attention to the food pantry, prior to the school board's formal action.

**4. The proposal does not model enrollment risk.**

The district's enrollment projections (HVL-EDU-001) do not account for the possibility that consolidation itself causes families to leave the district. This is a documented phenomenon in comparable consolidations. If consolidation results in even a 5–8% enrollment loss, the associated reduction in per-pupil state funding would offset a portion of the projected savings. Staff was unable to quantify this risk from available data.

**5. The attendance zone alternative was considered but not formally analyzed.**

Section 7 of the proposal discloses that attendance zone boundary adjustment was considered internally and dismissed without formal analysis. The dismissal rationale given — that boundary adjustment alone is insufficient to address the fiscal gap — may be correct, but council has not been provided the analysis to evaluate that claim.

Staff notes that boundary adjustment and consolidation are not mutually exclusive. A boundary adjustment that rebalances enrollment between the two schools could reduce the fiscal argument for closure while also addressing the class size concerns at Southside. Whether the resulting savings would be adequate is an empirical question the district has not answered.

**6. The disposition of the Southside building is unaddressed.**

The Southside Elementary property has an assessed value of $1,840,000. The proposal does not include a disposition plan or even a recommended process for developing one. Staff is aware that informal expressions of interest in the property have been received. Council may wish to establish a disposition process and criteria before the building is mothballed, rather than after.

---

### SUMMARY OF QUESTIONS FOR COUNCIL CONSIDERATION

Staff suggests the following questions as a framework for council deliberation:

1. Is the fiscal case for consolidation sufficiently documented, or should the council request independent verification before voting?

2. Should approval be conditioned on a specific capacity resolution commitment from the district?

3. What is the council's responsibility, if any, regarding community services displaced by closure? Is that a condition of approval or a separate process?

4. Should the council request a formal analysis of the attendance zone alternative before voting?

5. What process should govern disposition of the Southside building, and should that process be established before or after a closure vote?
