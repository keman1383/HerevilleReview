### OVERVIEW

The General Fund is the primary operating fund of the Town of Hereville and finances core municipal services including public safety, public works, parks and recreation, and general administration. This summary is prepared for public distribution in accordance with Virginia Code § 15.2-2503.

Total General Fund appropriations for FY2024–2025 are **$8,704,400**, representing a 2.1% increase over the prior fiscal year, driven primarily by personnel costs and debt service obligations.

### FUND BALANCE APPROPRIATION

Total revenues of $7,764,400 against total expenditures of $9,004,800 reflects a structural gap of **$1,240,400** in FY2024–25.

This gap is addressed through:
- Appropriation from unassigned fund balance: $900,000
- Anticipated year-end departmental lapses (estimated): $340,400

### NOTES TO BUDGET SUMMARY

**1. School Division Appropriation**
The Town's appropriation to Hereville City Schools is reflected under General Administration and totals $2,104,800 for FY2024–25. This represents the local share of school operating costs and is separate from state and federal education funding received directly by the school division. The school appropriation has remained flat for three consecutive fiscal years.

**2. Debt Service**
Debt service obligations increase through FY2027 due to the 2019 public works bond issuance for water line replacement on the north corridor. No additional bonding capacity is recommended at this time without corresponding revenue support.

**3. Personnel Costs**
Personnel costs across all departments increased 3.1% due to the state-mandated minimum wage adjustment and a negotiated 2% cost-of-living adjustment for public safety employees. No new positions were authorized in FY2024–25.

**4. Fund Balance Trend**
The unassigned fund balance has declined from $4,218,300 in FY2021–22 to a projected $1,760,200 at the close of FY2024–25, a reduction of approximately 58% over four fiscal years. The primary drivers are flat revenue growth, rising personnel and debt service costs, and one-time infrastructure expenditures not offset by new revenue.
