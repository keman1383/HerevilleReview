# TOWN OF HEREVILLE, VIRGINIA
## Economic Development Pipeline Assessment
**Document No.:** HVL-ECON-001  
**Prepared by:** Hereville Office of Community Development  
**Date:** August 2024  
**Distribution:** Public Record

---

### PURPOSE

This assessment provides a current summary of economic development activity in Hereville for the use of the town council and planning commission. It is updated annually. This edition reflects activity through July 31, 2024.

---

### ECONOMIC CONTEXT

Hereville's economy has not recovered to pre-bypass levels by any measurable indicator. Total employment within town limits is estimated at approximately 1,840 positions, compared to an estimated 2,980 in 1986, the year before the Route 220 bypass opened. Population has declined 31.2% over the same period.

The town's current economic base is anchored by healthcare (Blue Ridge Valley Medical Center, 12 miles east, is the largest employer of Hereville residents), retail trade, and a shrinking manufacturing sector. There is no major employer headquartered within town limits. The largest employer operating within Hereville is the school district, with approximately 142 employees.

The absence of a highway interchange directly serving Hereville's commercial core remains the single most significant structural constraint on economic development. Businesses dependent on visibility and access from Route 220 have located at interchange nodes outside town limits rather than in Hereville proper.

### ACTIVE DEVELOPMENT PROSPECTS

#### Prospect 1: Blue Ridge Logistics Partners — Distribution Facility
**Status: Exploratory. No commitment.**

Blue Ridge Logistics Partners, a regional third-party logistics firm headquartered in Roanoke, conducted a site visit to Hereville's Industrial Zone (east of town, I-1 zoning) in March 2024. The firm is evaluating locations for a small regional distribution hub, estimated at 60,000–80,000 square feet, that would employ approximately 35–50 workers at wages between $16–$22/hour.

Hereville competed against four other sites in a multi-county region. Staff's assessment is that the Hereville site is competitive on land cost and available acreage but faces disadvantages in road infrastructure (the industrial zone access road requires improvement estimated at $340,000–$480,000) and broadband reliability.

The firm's site selection decision is expected in Q1 2025. Staff has been in contact with the Virginia Economic Development Partnership, which has indicated that state incentive funding may be available if Hereville is selected.

**Realistic Employment Impact if Awarded:** 35–50 direct jobs. Indirect and induced employment effects would be modest given the scale of the facility.

**Realistic Tax Revenue Impact if Awarded (Year 3 stabilized):** Estimated $87,000–$112,000 annually in real property and machinery/tools taxes, subject to any negotiated incentive agreement.

**Timeline to Revenue:** 3–4 years from site selection to stabilized operations.

**Staff Assessment:** This is a genuine prospect at an early stage. It is not a certainty. If awarded, it would represent a meaningful but not transformative addition to the local economy. It would not resolve the town's structural fiscal gap within the relevant planning horizon.

---

#### Prospect 2: Heritage Tourism Initiative — Blue Ridge Crossroads Project
**Status: Grant-funded planning phase. Implementation uncertain.**

The Blue Ridge Crossroads Heritage Tourism Initiative is a regional collaboration among four localities, including Hereville, to develop heritage tourism infrastructure along a corridor connecting several historical sites. Hereville's assets include the former railroad depot (currently vacant, town-owned), several antebellum-era structures in the Garrison District, and proximity to existing regional trail networks.

The initiative received a $214,000 planning grant from the Virginia Tourism Corporation in 2022. A heritage tourism master plan was completed in spring 2024. The plan identifies Hereville as a potential "anchor community" on the corridor and recommends investment in wayfinding, depot renovation, and a visitor orientation center.

Implementation of the plan's recommendations would require an estimated $1.8M–$2.4M in capital investment, for which no funding has been identified. The plan projects that a fully developed heritage tourism presence could generate $380,000–$520,000 annually in visitor spending within Hereville, supporting an estimated 18–26 tourism-related jobs.

**Staff Assessment:** Heritage tourism is a realistic long-term complement to Hereville's economy, not a near-term solution. The planning work is credible. The funding gap is large. Implementation, if it occurs, is 5–8 years away from meaningful economic impact. Visitor spending projections for small heritage tourism destinations frequently exceed actual outcomes.

---

#### Prospect 3: Remote Worker Attraction Program
**Status: Concept. Not yet funded or launched.**

Staff has researched programs in comparable Virginia localities designed to attract remote workers by offering relocation incentives, emphasizing quality of life, affordable housing, and natural amenities. Several localities have implemented such programs with modest success.

A Hereville remote worker attraction program would require marketing investment (estimated $40,000–$60,000 to launch), broadband improvement in portions of town where connectivity is insufficient for reliable remote work, and a relocation incentive (comparable programs offer $5,000–$10,000 per household).

Staff estimates a successful program might attract 15–30 households over three years, generating modest increases in property tax revenue and local consumer spending.

**Staff Assessment:** Worth pursuing as a low-cost complement to other strategies. Not a significant economic driver on its own. Broadband investment is a prerequisite and is not currently funded.

### STRUCTURAL CONSTRAINTS ON ECONOMIC DEVELOPMENT

The following factors constrain Hereville's economic development prospects independent of any specific opportunity:

**Highway Access**
Hereville has no direct interstate access and is not visible from Route 220. Businesses requiring highway visibility or heavy truck access will consistently prefer alternative sites. This is not addressable without VDOT cooperation and significant capital investment, neither of which is currently in prospect.

**Workforce**
Hereville's labor force participation rate (54.8%) is well below the state average (65.2%). The working-age population has declined steadily. Employers considering Hereville must draw from a regional labor pool, which increases the importance of the highway access question.

**Broadband**
Approximately 22% of Hereville addresses lack access to broadband speeds adequate for business use or reliable remote work. This is a competitive disadvantage for both business attraction and remote worker recruitment. The town has applied for broadband expansion funding through the Virginia Telecommunications Initiative; a decision is pending.

**Building Stock**
The town has limited available commercial and industrial building stock in move-in condition. New construction economics are challenging given land values and construction costs relative to achievable rents. The most significant available commercial property is the former cannery building on the east side of town (currently privately owned, not listed for sale).

**Perception**
Businesses and households considering relocation frequently cite Hereville's trajectory of decline as a concern. Reversing this perception requires demonstrated momentum that does not currently exist. Each business closure and population loss makes the next recruitment more difficult.

### COMPARABLE COMMUNITIES

Staff reviewed economic development outcomes in six Virginia localities with similar characteristics — bypass-affected small towns with declining populations in the 3,500–6,000 range. Findings:

- Two localities stabilized population and achieved modest economic recovery through sustained regional cooperation, heritage tourism, and remote worker attraction over a 15–20 year period.
- Two localities continued to decline at rates comparable to Hereville's current trajectory.
- Two localities experienced significant economic events (one industrial recruitment, one university expansion nearby) that produced recovery not replicable through local policy alone.

**Staff Conclusion:** Hereville's economic trajectory is recoverable but not quickly, not through any single intervention, and not on a timeline relevant to the current fiscal year's budget decisions. Economic development strategy and fiscal stabilization are both necessary and must be pursued on parallel tracks. Economic development cannot substitute for near-term fiscal decisions.
