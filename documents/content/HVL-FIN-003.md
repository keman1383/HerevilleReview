### EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Under current revenue and expenditure trends, the Town of Hereville faces a structural fiscal gap that will exhaust the unassigned fund balance by FY2027–2028 absent corrective action. This outlook is prepared to inform council deliberations on pending policy decisions, including but not limited to school consolidation, infrastructure investment, and potential revenue measures.

**This document should be read in conjunction with HVL-FIN-001 and HVL-FIN-002.**

---

### REVENUE ASSUMPTIONS

- Real property tax: +0.7% annually (flat assessment base, modest new construction)
- Personal property tax: -1.5% annually (vehicle ownership declining with population)
- Local sales tax: -1.2% annually (continued retail contraction, no major new commercial activity)
- State aid: +1.4% Year 1 only; +0.8% thereafter (formula adjustment non-recurring)
- Federal pass-through: -2.0% annually (grant programs at risk of federal reduction)

No revenue assumptions reflect anticipated economic development activity. See HVL-ECON-001 for development pipeline assessment.

---

### EXPENDITURE PRESSURE POINTS

**Public Safety**
The fire department is currently operating with minimum safe staffing. The volunteer supplemental program has seen a 34% decline in active volunteers over five years. If volunteer participation continues to decline, the town will face pressure to convert 2–3 positions from part-time to full-time, adding an estimated $187,000–$204,000 annually.

**Debt Service**
Existing debt service obligations peak in FY2026–27 at an estimated $918,400 before beginning to decline. No new bonding is modeled in this projection.

**Infrastructure**
The town's water system infrastructure assessment (2023) identified $4.1M in deferred maintenance needs over ten years. The current capital improvement plan funds approximately $1.2M of this need. The remaining $2.9M is unfunded.

**Personnel**
State minimum wage increases are scheduled through 2026. Combined with anticipated health insurance premium increases of 6–8% annually, personnel costs are projected to increase 3.5–4.2% per year regardless of staffing level changes.

### SCENARIO MODELING — SCHOOL CONSOLIDATION IMPACT

The following reflects the baseline projection adjusted for school consolidation recurring savings of $829,500 annually beginning FY2025–26, with one-time transition costs of $400,000 absorbed in FY2025–26.

| Fiscal Year | Annual Gap (Baseline) | Consolidation Savings | Adjusted Gap | Est. Unassigned Balance EOY |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2024–25 | ($1,240,400) | — | ($1,240,400) | $1,760,200 |
| FY2025–26 | ($1,383,700) | +$829,500 | ($954,200) | $805,000 |
| FY2026–27 | ($1,531,600) | +$829,500 | ($702,100) | $102,900 |
| FY2027–28 | ($1,682,000) | +$829,500 | ($852,500) | ($749,600) |

> *School consolidation meaningfully extends the town's fiscal runway but does not resolve the structural gap. Under this scenario, fund balance falls below the recommended minimum in FY2026–27 and is exhausted by FY2027–28. Additional corrective action — revenue increases, further expenditure reductions, or both — will be required regardless of the consolidation decision.*

### OPTIONS NOT MODELED IN THIS DOCUMENT

The following revenue and expenditure options have been identified for potential council consideration but are not modeled here pending further direction:

1. Real property tax rate increase (currently $0.74/$100 assessed value; state average for comparable localities: $0.89)
2. Meals tax (currently none; enabling legislation in place; estimated yield at 4%: $310,000–$380,000 annually)
3. Reduction or restructuring of parks and recreation department
4. Consolidation of public works functions with adjacent locality (exploratory conversations only)
5. Pursuit of Virginia Community Development Block Grant for infrastructure needs

---

### STAFF RECOMMENDATION

The Town Manager does not make a recommendation on school consolidation as that is a policy decision within the purview of elected officials. Staff does recommend that council consider this five-year outlook in the context of any major expenditure or revenue decision made in the current fiscal year, and that a public work session on long-term fiscal sustainability be scheduled no later than Q2 FY2025–26.
