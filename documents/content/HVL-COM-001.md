**Comment 1**
*Submitted: September 17, 2024*
*Name: Patricia Osei*
*Address: 44 Dunmore Lane, Hereville*

I am writing as a parent of two children currently enrolled at Southside Elementary, grades 2 and 4. My family has lived in Hereville for eleven years. Both of my children have thrived at Southside. My youngest has Ms. Reyes for second grade and I genuinely believe she is the finest teacher either of my children has had.

My concern with consolidation is not primarily about the building. It is about what happens to the people. What happens to Ms. Reyes? What happens to the counselor my son has been seeing since last spring? What happens to the family liaison, Maria, who called me personally when my older daughter was struggling with the transition from third grade? These are not abstract resources. They are people who know my children.

I have attended two school board meetings and one town council work session on this topic. At none of these meetings has anyone from the district or the town been able to tell me specifically what happens to these staff members. I would like an answer before a vote is taken.

**Comment 5**
*Submitted: September 25, 2024*
*Name: Anne Whitfield*
*Address: 90 Sycamore Court, Hereville*

I am a retired educator with 28 years in Virginia public schools, the last twelve as a principal in this district. I want to offer a few observations based on that experience.

School consolidations frequently fail to achieve their projected savings because transition costs are underestimated and enrollment effects are not modeled honestly. When families perceive that a school closure is being done to their community rather than for their children, some of those families leave the district. Each student who leaves takes their per-pupil state funding with them. I would ask the council to request an independent analysis of enrollment risk before voting.

I would also note that Southside Elementary's test score gap disappears almost entirely when you account for student poverty levels, as the VDOE equity profile found. Closing a school because its scores look lower when they aren't actually lower is not sound policy.

I am not opposed to consolidation in principle. I am opposed to a rushed consolidation without honest accounting of the full costs.

**Comment 7**
*Submitted: October 1, 2024*
*Name: Sandra Pruitt*
*Address: 309 Maple Avenue, Hereville*

I am the coordinator of the Southside Food Pantry. We have served this community since 2011, operating out of Southside Elementary with the full support of the school administration and the generous partnership of the district.

Last year we distributed food to 312 families. Many of those families have children at Southside Elementary. Many do not. We serve seniors. We serve working families who are one unexpected bill away from not having enough to eat. We serve people who would never walk into a church or a government office to ask for help, but who will come to the school.

I want to be direct with the council: we have no place to go. I have spent the past two months looking. There is no available commercial space in our price range in Hereville that has a commercial kitchen and adequate storage. The closest option I have found is a former restaurant space on Route 220 that is available for $2,400 per month, has no kitchen equipment, and is not accessible by foot or transit from most of our clients.

We are a small organization. We do not have the reserves to survive a six-month gap in operations. I am asking the council to make the future of our organization a specific, answered question before the vote, not an afterthought.

**Comment 11**
*Submitted: October 14, 2024*
*Name: Jennifer Adkins, LCSW*
*Address: 204 Valley View Road, Hereville*

I am a licensed clinical social worker. I contract with Hereville City Schools to provide counseling services, including at Southside Elementary where I see students one day per week.

I want to flag something that may not be visible in the budget documents: school transitions are hard for children, and they are hardest for children who are already carrying difficult circumstances. Several of my Southside clients have experienced housing instability, family separation, or trauma. For these children, the school building and the people in it represent consistency and safety.

I am not saying this means the school cannot close. I am saying that if it does, there needs to be a real transition plan for vulnerable students that is more than reassigning them to a different building and hoping for the best.
