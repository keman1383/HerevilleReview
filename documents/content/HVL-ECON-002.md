# TOWN OF HEREVILLE, VIRGINIA
## Economic Development Efforts Since 1987: A Summary Record
**Document No.:** HVL-ECON-002  
**Prepared by:** Hereville Office of Community Development  
**Sources:** Town Council minutes 1987–2024; Office of Community Development project files; Virginia Economic Development Partnership records  
**Date:** June 2024  
**Distribution:** Public Record

---

### PURPOSE

This document summarizes economic development initiatives undertaken by the Town of Hereville since the opening of the Route 220 bypass in 1987. It is intended to provide historical context for current planning and to document the outcomes of past efforts for the benefit of current and future elected officials and staff.

---

### OVERVIEW

In the 37 years since the bypass opened, Hereville has pursued economic development through a range of strategies, including business recruitment, downtown revitalization, tourism, and regional cooperation. Results have been modest. No single initiative has reversed or significantly slowed the town's economic decline. Several initiatives produced limited but genuine benefits. Some produced no measurable outcome.

This record is not intended as a criticism of past officials or staff. Economic development in bypass-affected small towns is genuinely difficult, and Hereville's experience is consistent with comparable localities in Virginia and across the region.

---

### INITIATIVE RECORD — SELECTED ENTRIES

---

**1989–1992: Downtown Revitalization Facade Program**
*Outcome: Partial success*

Following the bypass, Main Street vacancy rates increased from approximately 8% to 31% between 1987 and 1989. The town established a facade improvement grant program using Community Development Block Grant funds, providing matching grants to building owners for exterior improvements. Fourteen properties participated. Vacancy rates stabilized but did not return to pre-bypass levels. Three grant recipients subsequently sold or vacated their properties. The program was discontinued in 1992 when CDBG funding was redirected to water infrastructure.

---

**1994–1996: Hereville Industrial Recruitment Initiative**
*Outcome: Failed*

The town engaged a Richmond-based economic development consultant to recruit light manufacturing or distribution employers to Hereville's industrial zone. The consultant identified twelve prospects over two years. None selected Hereville. Common objections cited in prospect feedback: highway access, workforce availability, and competition from better-resourced localities with state incentive packages Hereville could not match. The consultant contract was not renewed. Total expenditure: $84,000.

---

**1998: Textiles Plant — Garrison Manufacturing*
*Outcome: Short-term success, long-term failure*

Garrison Manufacturing, a textile contractor, opened a 40-worker facility in the Hereville industrial zone in 1998 following negotiation of a five-year real property tax abatement. The facility operated at or near capacity through 2002. In 2003, Garrison Manufacturing relocated operations to a facility in North Carolina and subsequently offshored production. The facility has been vacant since 2004. The town's tax abatement recovered no long-term revenue benefit.

---

**2001–2004: Heritage Tourism Pilot**
*Outcome: Negligible*

The town invested $38,000 in wayfinding signage, a visitor brochure, and a modest website promoting Hereville's history and natural amenities. Visitor counts at the town's designated attractions did not increase measurably. Staff attributed this to the absence of a flagship attraction, inadequate marketing budget relative to regional competitors, and the lack of lodging options within town limits. The initiative was not formally discontinued but received no further funding after 2004.

---

**2006: Retail Recruitment — National Chain Discussions**
*Outcome: Failed*

Town staff and council members held discussions with representatives of two national retail chains regarding locations in Hereville. Both chains declined, citing population thresholds below their minimum site criteria. Staff was advised that Hereville's population would need to exceed approximately 7,500 for most national retail chains to consider a location — roughly double the current population.

---

**2009–2011: Main Street Program Participation**
*Outcome: Modest success*

Hereville participated in the Virginia Main Street program from 2009 to 2011, receiving technical assistance in organizational development, design, promotion, and economic restructuring. The program supported the opening of four small businesses on Main Street during the participation period. Two of those businesses remain in operation. The town did not continue participation after the initial grant period due to the required local match.

---

**2014: Regional Cooperation Study — Shared Services with Adjacent County**
*Outcome: Partial implementation*

A jointly commissioned study examined opportunities for shared services between Hereville and the surrounding county. The study identified potential savings in public works, emergency dispatch, and school administration. Emergency dispatch was consolidated in 2015, saving the town approximately $67,000 annually — the most financially significant economic/administrative initiative of the post-bypass period. Public works and school administration consolidation were not pursued following disagreement over cost-sharing formulas.

---

**2016–2018: Broadband Expansion Effort**
*Outcome: Partial success*

The town participated in a regional broadband cooperative that extended fiber service to approximately 60% of Hereville addresses. Funding through the Virginia Telecommunications Initiative covered the majority of infrastructure costs. The remaining 40% of addresses — concentrated in the older housing stock on the south side and in rural fringe areas — were not served. A second phase application was submitted in 2022 and remains pending.

---

**2019–2020: Remote Worker Recruitment Pilot**
*Outcome: Inconclusive*

Following the success of similar programs in West Virginia and Vermont, the town allocated $22,000 for a pilot remote worker recruitment campaign emphasizing affordable housing and mountain proximity. Twelve households inquired; four relocated to Hereville. Two of those households remained in Hereville as of 2023. Staff considers the pilot inconclusive due to the small sample size and the confounding effects of the COVID-19 pandemic on remote work patterns.

### OBSERVATIONS

Several patterns are evident across this record:

**External factors dominate outcomes.** The initiatives with the most significant impacts — Garrison Manufacturing's opening and closure, the emergency dispatch consolidation, the broadband expansion — were shaped primarily by factors outside local control: national economic trends, regional cooperation dynamics, and state funding availability. Local initiative mattered at the margins.

**Small towns compete at a structural disadvantage.** Hereville consistently lost business recruitment competitions to localities with better highway access, larger workforces, or greater capacity to offer incentive packages. This is not a failure of effort; it is a feature of Hereville's structural position that has not changed.

**Tourism and downtown revitalization produce quality-of-life benefits but limited economic growth.** These strategies improve the experience of living in Hereville and may contribute to modest retention and attraction of residents. They do not produce employment or tax revenue at a scale sufficient to alter the town's fiscal trajectory.

**Incremental gains have not compounded.** None of the successful initiatives produced conditions that made the next initiative more likely to succeed. The town's economic position in 2024 is not significantly better than it was in 2004, despite sustained effort.

---

### STAFF NOTE

This document is not intended to discourage economic development effort. It is intended to provide an honest baseline for evaluating current proposals and expectations. Staff recommends that economic development projections presented to the council be evaluated against this record before being incorporated into fiscal planning.
